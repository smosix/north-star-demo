Questions people ask

What is virtual work experience?

Online work experience where you try a real job through films, quizzes and simulations designed with employers. Each of the 3 VWEs runs as a 10-day placement for KS4, KS5 and T Level learners, or a 30-minute career taster that spotlights one career. You earn a certificate and an evidence report you can use to apply. See the VWEs.

What is CETEC?

The Clean Energy Technical Excellence College for the East. It is led by Colchester Institute, with Suffolk New College, East Coast College and City College Norwich as core delivery partners, and works with industry partners including Sizewell C, EDF, Freeport East, EEEGR and EastWind. Its focus is offshore wind, nuclear, solar, domestic renewables and grid infrastructure.

Why the East of England?

The East coast hosts a large share of the UK’s offshore wind, a new nuclear power station at Sizewell C, the UK’s biggest container port at Felixstowe and growing solar, storage and grid projects. These all need skilled people, many of them locally.

When will the jobs start?

Many are open now, especially in offshore wind operations, electrical work and nuclear construction. More will follow as new wind farms, Sizewell C and grid upgrades move through build into decades of operation.

Do I need to work offshore?

No. Many clean energy jobs are onshore: in ports, factories, control rooms, offices, labs, substations and people’s homes. Offshore roles need safety training such as GWO and a medical, which employers often help with.

Do I need a degree?

No. Many roles start with an apprenticeship, a T Level or on-the-job training, including wind turbine technician, electrician, welder, port operative and solar installer. Engineering can also be reached by a degree apprenticeship, so you earn while you learn.

I'm an adult changing career. Is it too late?

No. Apprenticeships have no upper age limit, and skills from trades, driving, the forces, fishing, and oil and gas count. Ask a CETEC college about adult courses and Skills Bootcamps, and see the crossover routes.

      Money words explained

      Gross pay

      Your pay before anything is taken off.

      Take-home (net) pay

      What reaches your bank account after tax, National Insurance, pension and any student loan.

      Personal allowance

      The first £12,570 you earn each year is tax-free.

      Income tax

      20% on earnings from £12,570 to £50,270 (basic rate), 40% above (higher rate). Your tax code tells your employer how much allowance you get, usually 1257L.

      National Insurance

      8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270, then 2%. It counts towards your State Pension.

      Pension

      Money saved for later life. In the NHS Pension Scheme you pay 5.2% to 12.5% and your employer adds 23.7%. Other employers must add at least 3%.

      Student loan

      You only repay once you earn over the threshold: 9% of earnings above £29,385 (Plan 2) or £25,000 (Plan 5). It stops when you earn less, and is written off after 30 or 40 years.

      Shift and offshore allowances

      Extra pay some energy jobs add for nights, rotas or working offshore. It varies by employer.

      Unsocial hours

      Extra NHS pay for evenings, nights, weekends and bank holidays, as a percentage of your hourly rate.

      Overtime

      Hours worked above your normal week. In construction it is often paid at time and a half.

      Council tax

      A local tax on your home, paid monthly to the council. If you live alone you get 25% off.

      Lifetime ISA

      A savings account for a first home for people aged 18 to 39. The government adds 25% to up to £4,000 a year.

      Estimates only, not financial advice. Minimum wage rates are the GOV.UK rates from 1 April 2026; the Real Living Wage of £14.10 was announced by the Living Wage Foundation on 15 October 2026. Pension projections are illustrative, in today’s money, and not guaranteed. All pay is an example range for the East of England, to be confirmed with employers, with a 5% auto-enrolment pension and a 3% employer contribution (many energy employers pay more). Overtime and offshore allowances are rough estimates. Take-home uses 2026/27 income tax and National Insurance for England with the standard personal allowance, and 2026/27 student loan thresholds. Rents, council tax and house prices are rounded typical figures for each energy town from ONS and local listings in 2026; check local listings. Mortgage figures assume a 5% deposit, 30 years and 4.5% interest; real offers depend on your circumstances. Your choices are saved in this browser only.

          How we’ll make it work

          Eight commitments that turn interest into jobs

          Built around what CETEC needs most: more employer contact without more strain on employers, a real next step for every learner, and evidence that it works.

          1

          One day from each employer, reused everywhere

          Each anchor employer gives one day of filming and one set of tasks. All four CETEC colleges and their feeder schools then reuse it, year after year. Employer contact for thousands of learners, without thousands of placements.

          2

          A guaranteed next step after every VWE

          Every completed VWE ends with a real offer agreed in advance: an interview, a site or port visit, a taster day or a CETEC open day. We track how many learners take it up.

          3

          Microcredentials matched to industry tickets

          Credentials line up with what employers ask for on day one: GWO basic safety awareness for offshore wind, CSCS preparation for sites, and nuclear safety culture for Sizewell. Employers validate them, and they cut time and cost at induction.

          4

          An adult and career-change route

          The same VWEs for adults: oil and gas workers moving into offshore wind, veterans, people out of work and NEET young people. Linked to CETEC adult courses, Skills Bootcamps and Jobcentre Plus. See the adult route →

          5

          T Level placement preparation

          Each VWE prepares learners for T Level industry placements, which are scarce in clean energy. Whether VWE hours can count towards a placement will be explored with DfE before any promise is made.

          6

          Measurement built in

          Before and after surveys on every VWE, tracking from VWE to course application to apprenticeship start, inclusion targets, and an independent evaluation. See the measures →

          7

          Data and safeguarding answered up front

          Learners choose what to share. Evidence reports reach employers only with the learner’s consent, and with a parent’s consent for under-16s. Data protection impact assessment, safeguarding policy and data-sharing agreements are agreed with each college before launch.

          8

          Phased, so it proves itself first

          Build and test each VWE with its anchor employer and a first group of schools and college learners, then roll it out across the four CETEC colleges, their feeder schools and the adult route.

          Step 1 · co-designBuild with employersEach VWE is filmed and tested with its anchor employer, appointed by College on the Coast, Suffolk New College and Sizewell C. The build takes about four months once the employer is engaged.
          Step 2 · testFirst cohortsRun with a first group of schools and CETEC college learners. Results reviewed with the CETEC steering group.
          Step 3 · roll outAcross the regionAll four colleges, their feeder schools, T Level learners and the adult route.

          In-demand roles

          Which jobs does the region need most?

          Each role gets a demand score out of 100 from five signals: live vacancies, what the region’s energy projects need, workforce gaps, retirements and how few people are training for it. Use it to choose a VWE with strong job prospects, or to plan training and recruitment.

          Illustrative scores. Live vacancies come from the jobs board; the other signals are example values. On the live site they come from the government’s Clean Energy Jobs Plan (31 national priority occupations), the Local Skills Improvement Plans for Norfolk and Suffolk and for Essex, live vacancy data and the industry partners’ workforce plans. “National priority” marks roles named on the Clean Energy Jobs Plan list.

          All roles, ranked

          How the score works, and adjust it

          Each signal is scored 0 to 100, then combined using the weights below. Move a slider to change how much a signal counts. Critical is 72 or more, High 63 to 71, Rising 54 to 62, and anything lower is Steady.

          Data sources for the live system

          • Live vacancies: the partner feeds on this site (Find an apprenticeship, cluster member feeds, partner careers sites), plus regional job-postings data
          • Programme need: employers' labour plans for Sizewell C, offshore wind, grid and port projects, by trade and year
          • Workforce gap: EastWind workforce research, CITB Construction Skills Network forecasts and nuclear sector workforce data for the East of England
          • Retirement risk: workforce age profiles from employers; industry age data
          • Pipeline: VWE completions on this site, plus CETEC college, apprenticeship and university starts
          • Local priorities: the Local Skills Improvement Plans for Norfolk and Suffolk, and Essex, Southend and Thurrock
