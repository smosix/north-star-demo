Module 3 · Lesson 3.2 · about 15 minutes

Checking AI output

By the end you'll be able to

Check an AI-written message for mistakes and private details before it is sent.

  1. 1 Read the message
  2. 2 Find the mistakes
  3. 3 Say how to fix them

What this is about

AI tools write quickly and sound confident, but they can be wrong. At work, you are the person who checks before anything is sent or saved.

Three questions to ask every time

Is it true? Check facts and numbers against a trusted source.
Is it safe? Look for personal details that should not be there.
Is it right for this person? Check the tone and that it answers what was asked.

If the job does not work out

  • Universal Credit is worked out each month from what you earn. If your hours drop or the job ends, your payment goes back up from the next monthly assessment, as long as your claim is open.
  • If you earn enough to get no Universal Credit, your claim can usually stay open for up to six months, so it restarts without a new claim. Check this with your work coach.
  • PIP is not affected by earnings or hours, so it carries on whether you work or not.
  • New Style ESA: tell DWP before you start any work, and ask your work coach what happens if you stop.

Right to try

Since the end of April 2026, starting work or volunteering does not on its own trigger a reassessment of PIP, New Style ESA or the Universal Credit health element.

Check the latest rules on GOV.UK or with your work coach before relying on this.

Which might apply to me?

A starting point for your conversation with a work coach or benefits adviser. Your own circumstances decide what you can get.

If you can work and are looking for a job

New Style JSA may be an option if you meet the National Insurance contribution conditions.

If your condition limits how much you can work

New Style ESA may be more appropriate, depending on your National Insurance record.

If you need help with living costs

Universal Credit may be available. A Work Capability Assessment decides whether you qualify for extra health-related support.

If you get PIP

You can generally work while receiving it, as long as you still meet the eligibility criteria. Earnings do not affect it.

Guide for course writersWriting for the Can-Do Helper

The Can-Do Helper does not make up its own teaching. It works from what you write for each lesson. These seven parts tell it how to explain, hint, encourage and when to pass a learner to their job coach. The editor below has a box for each one.

  1. 1. Simple version

    One or two short sentences in plain English, written for a busy adult reading on a phone. The helper uses this when a learner says "I don't get it".

  2. 2. Hints

    Two hints, from a gentle nudge to almost the answer. The helper never gives the answer before the learner has tried.

  3. 3. Job example

    A real example from work. Write {job} and {employer} and the helper fills in the learner's own North Star job.

  4. 4. Common mistake

    The mistake most learners make, and how to correct it clearly and respectfully.

  5. 5. Feedback

    What to say when they get it right: confirm it briefly and link it to a skill on their North Star.

  6. 6. Key words

    Any word a learner might not know, with a plain meaning. One per line: word = meaning.

  7. 7. Pass to the coach

    Topics where the helper should stop and suggest messaging the job coach, such as health, money worries or feeling unsafe.

Tone: adult and direct. Learners are working at Level 3. Use "you", one idea per sentence, no jargon, and no over-praise. The plain-English checks below apply to the helper's words too.

Before you send for review: press Test the helper and read the conversation out loud. It should read like a knowledgeable colleague: clear, direct and respectful. Avoid praise that sounds like it is written for children.

Sorting and filtering a spreadsheet

By the end you'll be able to: Sort and filter a stock list to find items that are running low.

Spreadsheets hold lists of information, like stock or rotas. Sorting puts them in order. Filtering hides what you do not need, so you can focus.

  • Sort to put things in order, like A to Z.
  • Filter to show only some rows.
  • Always check the total before and after.

Proof of concept · Turning Factor · in person, non-disabled learners

The route works in person. Now we test it with disabled people.

Success means more than a job start: each person happy, confident and ready to progress and thrive at work.

In the in-person proof of concept, 7 applied, 3 were accepted, and all 3 were placed and are still in work. It was classroom-based and not designed for disabled learners. Can-Do applies that training-to-placement route to a new group: disabled people and people with long-term health conditions in Norfolk, online, fully accessible and tied to a named employer need.

Phase 1 · 12-month grant · 50 participants

What phase 1 aims to achieve

Targets from the bid, across the four stages the Fund asks about: moving closer to work, entering work, staying in work and making progress.

From screening to sustained work

Screening of Universal Credit claimants through Connect to Work and WorkWell. Cohorts of 10 start in months 2 to 7.

Months 2 to 8

Moving closer to work

About 1 in 3 of those pre-selected engage, and 50 enrol. 80% (40) complete the Level 3 qualification.

Months 4 to 12

Entering work

40 placements and 30 job starts (60%), mostly in posts created by host employers. 95% with adjustments in place on day one.

Month 7 onwards

Staying in work

85% still in work at three months and 75% at six months. 70% target at 12 months.

In work

Making progress

Taking on extra responsibilities or an AI-related role, evidenced by employer feedback.

What the independent evaluation tests

The University of East Anglia compares results with matched disabled people on standard support. Findings go to DWP quarterly, and the final report includes what did not work.

QuestionWhat we tryHow we measure it
Does an employer-matched Level 3 AI route improve job entry and retention?Match each learner to a host employer with a named AI task, and design the placement to become a new post.Job starts and retention at 3, 6 and 12 months, compared with matched disabled people on standard support.
Which matching approaches and adjustments work best?Match on interests, strengths and support needs. Employers set out adjustments and Access to Work support before matching.Placements that become posts, adjustments in place on day one, and early exits.
Does Virtual Work Experience reduce anxiety and improve attendance?Every candidate tries the employer's tasks online before day one.Self-rated confidence before and after, and placement attendance.
Do a better-off calculation and early Access to Work prevent lost job offers?A better-off calculation before any placement, and Access to Work started at matching, with interim adjustments from the employer.Offers lost or turned down, money worries reported, and days from matching to adjustments in place.
Which candidate profiles and employers lead to sustained employment?Record each participant's route, support and employer, with consent.Sustained employment by disability, health condition, employer and support received.

Measures, recorded for every participant

Outcomes are reviewed by disability and health condition, with consent and suitable safeguards.

  • Referrals, engagement and enrolment, and why people do not start
  • Level 3 completion, from platform records and qualification results
  • Confidence self-assessments during the course and before the placement
  • Placements, new posts created and contract starts, confirmed by employers
  • Adjustments in place on the first day
  • Retention at 3, 6 and 12 months (12 months collected by Turning Factor at its own cost after the grant)
  • Progression: extra responsibilities or an AI-related role, from employer feedback and examples of workplace improvements
  • Employers: at least one AI process adopted, and confidence in recruiting disabled people

Lived experience

Shaped by disabled people

In October 2026, Turning Factor interviewed eight disabled claimants by telephone: four receiving PIP and four receiving Universal Credit. Each barrier they described changed the design.

What claimants told usWhat Can-Do doesWhere in the demo
Travelling to a venue and sitting in a classroom for hours leaves some people in too much pain to learn.All learning online, in lessons of 15 minutes or less, with progress saved and no time limits.
Some people can only manage support on certain days.Tutor follow-up in person or by phone, at times that suit the learner.
"When I have a question and I can get help, I can do it."Simpler content with pictures, an "Ask a person" button, and tutor follow-up after every session.
Routines are hard to keep.One weekly plan of study, placement and appointments, with reminders the learner chooses.
New workplaces are daunting.Virtual Work Experience introduces the employer's tasks before day one.
Fear of losing benefits.A better-off calculation, reviewed by a benefits adviser, before any placement.
"The Access to Work process takes so long I lose the job offer."Applications begin at matching, with interim adjustments from the employer.
Not knowing what support an employer will give.Every host employer sets out its adjustments and Access to Work support before matching.

How disabled people stay involved

  • A focus group on 16 November and a scoping workshop on 17 November 2026, with Future Projects, refine the design before delivery
  • The focus group tests this prototype and helps decide which features are developed in phase 2
  • A paid panel of disabled people meets monthly, approves each release and holds a seat on the steering group
  • Disabled testers check every release against WCAG 2.2 AA
  • Employer feedback complements learner views, never replaces them
