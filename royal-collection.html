Skills delivery · Access · Impact reporting

Building the skills that care for the Royal Collection

Four Developing Experts virtual work experiences (VWEs) bring 32 Royal Collection careers into classrooms, colleges and communities. Every VWE builds practical skills, produces an evidence report for each learner and feeds termly impact data back to Royal Collection Trust.

What RCT needs

Skills delivery that reaches further and proves its impact

Specialist skills

Fill specialist roles

The Royal Bindery, founded in 1770, and RCT’s conservation teams depend on rare hand skills. The Queen’s Bindery Apprenticeship Scheme was suspended in 2020, three years early.

VWEs introduce learners to each role early and give recruiters an evidence report to judge applicants by.

Access

Reach pupils who face barriers

The Access Scheme and outreach in Windsor, Slough, Maidenhead and Staines already target pupils who face distance and cost barriers.

VWEs run in any classroom, all year, free to Access Scheme schools. A visit becomes the finale, not the only contact.

Evidence

Show sponsors the return

RCT receives no public funding, and its net income fell by more than £10m in the latest accounts. Sponsors and trustees need evidence.

Impact reports show reach, skills gained and progression each term, by VWE, area and group.

Developing Experts history curriculum · KS1 and KS2

History lessons that lead to the Collection

Developing Experts’ primary history curriculum has 18 units across Years 1 to 6, fully aligned to the National Curriculum. Every lesson starts with an enquiry question, key learning comes back again in later units, and every unit includes career links. Seven units connect directly to the Royal Collection, so pupils meet its people and places long before they reach a VWE.

In every unitLesson plans, presentations, animations, key words, differentiated handouts and a knowledge organiser
AssessmentFormative questions, end-of-lesson quizzes, and an open-ended summative task in lesson 6
CareersCareer links in every unit, which can feature Royal Collection roles from the VWEs
AvailabilityThe full curriculum launches in late 2026. Castles is one of three free sample units.

How VWEs are made

Every five-day VWE goes through the same ten phases, from choosing the roles to publishing, with approval gates where Royal Collection Trust signs off. It takes about 14 weeks from first meeting to launch.

      What one five-day placement produces

      • Five days of filmed story, about 25 hours of learning
      • Quizzes, simulations, 360° tours and skills drills, with the AI Tutor alongside
      • Eight 30-minute KS3 career tasters, one for each role
      • Teacher training films
      • An evidence report for every learner, usable as a recruitment screen
      • Learner and impact data in RCT’s impact dashboard
      See the VWE library →

      What Royal Collection Trust commits

      Indicative, to agree in phase 2.

      • Job holders for each of the eight featured roles: a 30-minute interview and a filming day
      • Brand and marketing: a planning meeting, intros, outros and asset sign-off
      • Security: script clearance and footage sign-off
      • Health and safety: filming permissions, passes and an induction for the crew
      • Communications: agree the press release
